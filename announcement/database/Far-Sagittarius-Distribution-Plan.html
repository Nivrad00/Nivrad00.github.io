Far Sagittarius Distribution Plan

Article Discussion

The Scutum-Centaurus Arm and Sagittarius Arm, as seen from Earth.

The Far Sagittarius Distribution Plan (FSDP) was a multi-corporate contract signed on November 15, 2082, which determined ownership of planets in the Far Sagittarius region. It was facilitated by the Pan-Galactic Trade Organization and signed by LumiTrust, StarPAC, NoTo, and 15 smaller corporations. The FSDP is generally regarded as a failure, as only five out of the six hundred original claims were ever successfully colonized.

Background

The 2070's saw the development of second generation FTL engines, which improved space travel speeds by 1000-fold. Corporations rushed to claim planets in regions of the Milky Way previously thought to be inaccessible[8], in what is now known as the Second Galactic Rush.

The FSDP was the most ambitious of the many planetary claim contracts created in this era, targeting exoplanets in the farther half of the Sagittarius Arm. The farthest claim made was the planet G-202, 60 kilolight-years from Earth, which would have nearly doubled the record for most remote colony at the time. The FSDP's budget was largely funded by FTL engine manufacturers and reached 30 trillion USD between all participating administrations[10].

Implementation and failure

The FSDP started to roll out in 2085, with SpacePAC establishing Far Sagittarius's first major colony, New Gujarat. Other successful settlements included the LK-15 research base and the LXC-2152b mining colony. However, progress quickly halted due to a series of technological failures and natural disasters (see the New Gujarat Solar Storm of 2098). Many detractors of the FSDP blame corporations' shortcut-taking, claiming FSDP missions were launched long before the sufficient development of second-gen FTL infrastructure[12].

After the optimism of the Second Galactic Rush had passed, corporations were generally reluctant to invest in extra-remote missions like the FSDP. Thus, the Far Sagittarius colonies remain the most remote settlements in the Milky Way to this day.
